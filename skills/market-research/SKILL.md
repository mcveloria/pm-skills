# Market Research

Always state sources and confidence. Never invent figures: if data is missing, say so, give a labeled assumption, and show how to verify it.

## Workflow

1. **Clarify the decision.** What will this research change (enter market, prioritize segment, price, position)? Scope to that.
2. **Gather.** Use web search, filings, analyst reports, review sites (G2, Capterra, app stores), job posts, pricing pages, and customer calls. Record source and date for every fact.
3. **Analyze** with the frameworks below.
4. **Synthesize** into: key findings, implications, risks, recommendation, open questions.

## Market sizing

- **Top-down:** industry revenue x relevant share. Quick but flattering.
- **Bottom-up (preferred):** `# target accounts x adoption rate x annual price`. Show every input.
- Report **TAM** (all demand), **SAM** (reachable with your model and geography), **SOM** (realistic share in 3 years).
- Triangulate with at least two methods. If they differ by more than 2x, explain why.

## Competitive analysis

Build a table per competitor: positioning, target customer, core features, pricing and packaging, distribution, strengths, weaknesses, recent moves, evidence links.

Include three competitor types: **direct**, **indirect** (different product, same job), and **status quo** (spreadsheets, agencies, doing nothing).

Then produce:
- **Feature/value matrix** (rows = customer needs, not feature names)
- **Positioning map** on two axes that matter to buyers
- **Where we win / lose / are at parity**, each backed by evidence
- **Battlecard** for sales: landmines, objection handling, proof points

## Segmentation and personas

Segment on behavior and need (job, trigger, budget, urgency), not only demographics. Score segments on size, growth, pain intensity, accessibility, and fit with our strengths. Persona = goals, triggers, objections, buying committee, channels. Mark which details are researched vs assumed.

## Other frameworks (use when relevant)

Porter's Five Forces for industry structure, PESTEL for macro risk, SWOT only as a summary of evidence, Wardley mapping for build/buy, win/loss interviews for real buying reasons.

## Output template

```
Decision this informs:

Market size (method, inputs, range):
Segments ranked:

Implications for us:

```

## Pitfalls

Treating TAM as opportunity, copying competitor roadmaps, ignoring the status quo, using stale data (flag anything older than 18 months), and survivorship bias in customer quotes.
