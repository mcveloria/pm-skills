# Product Discovery

Goal: reduce the risk of building the wrong thing. Cover four risks for every idea: **value** (will they want it), **usability** (can they use it), **feasibility** (can we build it), **viability** (does it work for the business).

## Workflow

1. **Frame the problem.** Ask for: target customer, the situation or trigger, the current workaround, and why now. Write a one-line problem statement: `[Who] struggles to [job] when [situation], because [barrier], which costs them [impact].`
2. **Define the desired outcome.** One measurable outcome (a metric that moves from X to Y), not a feature.
3. **Plan research.** Pick the cheapest method that answers the riskiest question (see table). Draft a screener and an interview guide.
4. **Synthesize.** Cluster observations into needs, pains, and desired outcomes. Tag each with evidence strength (quote, behavior seen, or opinion).
5. **Map opportunities.** Build an opportunity solution tree: outcome → opportunities (customer needs) → solutions → assumption tests.
6. **Test assumptions.** List assumptions, rank by impact x uncertainty, and design the smallest test for the top 2-3.
7. **Decide.** Output a recommendation: pursue, pivot, or kill, with the evidence behind it.

## Method picker

| Question | Method |
|---|---|
| Does this problem exist and matter? | Problem interviews (5-8), survey to size |
| How do they do it today? | Contextual inquiry, diary study |
| Will they want this solution? | Fake door, landing page, concierge test |
| Can they use it? | Prototype usability test (5 users) |
| Will they pay? | Pre-sell, pricing interviews, Van Westendorp |

## Interview rules (The Mom Test)

- Ask about past behavior, not future intent. "Tell me about the last time you..." beats "Would you use...".
- Never pitch in a problem interview. Dig for specifics, money spent, time lost, workarounds built.
- Ask "what else?" and "why?" at least twice. Silence is a tool.
- Compliments and hypotheticals are noise. Commitments (time, money, intros) are signal.

### Interview guide template

1. Warm-up and context (role, team, tools)
2. Last time they did the job: walk through step by step
3. Pain points, workarounds, what they tried
4. Cost of the problem (time, money, risk)
5. Decision process: who else is involved, what triggers a change
6. Wrap: "Who else should I talk to?"

## Jobs to be done

Format: `When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].` Capture functional, emotional, and social dimensions. Competing "solutions" include doing nothing and spreadsheets.

## Assumption testing

For each assumption write: belief, evidence so far, test, success threshold set **before** running, owner, deadline. Example threshold: "At least 8 of 30 visitors click the fake-door CTA."

## Outputs

Offer any of: problem statement, interview guide, screener, synthesis doc (themes, quotes, evidence strength), opportunity solution tree (text outline), assumption map, discovery readout. Always end with the riskiest unanswered question and the next test.

## Pitfalls to flag

Leading questions, sample of friendly users only, mistaking feature requests for needs, discovering once instead of continuously (aim for weekly customer touchpoints).
